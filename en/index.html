How we measured it

Zas 1.15.1, the version you download here, on a PC with an AMD Ryzen 5 3600, an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 2060, 32 GB of memory and Windows 11, with a 2560×1440 screen. Test video: one minute of Big Buck Bunny (H.264, 1080p at 60 fps) and two 10 s versions made from it (H.265 in 4K at 30 fps and VP9 1080p at 30 fps).

Open: from launching zas.exe video (the same as a double-click) to the first frame on screen. Medians of 8 opens in memory and 5 from scratch. With Zas in memory, 2 of 24 opens took longer (0.7 and 2.9 s); with Zas closed, the slowest was 0.83 s. The file was already in the Windows cache.

Clip copied: marking a 12 s part and pressing Ctrl+E (no re-encoding) until the file is on the clipboard. Median of 5 runs: 0.14 s; the first took 33 s and is unexplained. Frame-accurate (re-encoding, Ctrl+Shift+E), the same part takes 4.6 to 5.0 s.

Memory: 99 MB idle (in the background, ready to open) and about 300 MB playing the 1080p60 video.

These are measurements from one specific computer: yours may differ.

A clip in seconds

From video to chat in three keys.

  1. I O Mark the part

    Start and end, while you watch.

  2. Ctrl E Export

    No re-encoding: the clip is ready right away, at the original quality.

  3. Ctrl V Paste

    It’s already on the clipboard: paste it into any chat.

One app, not three

Everything you do with your videos.

Play

It stays in memory after closing, so the next video shows up as soon as you open it. The interface disappears when you’re not using it and it plays almost any format. It’s also a fast photo viewer: JPEG, PNG, WebP, HEIC, AVIF and more.

Edit

Split and remove parts on the timeline. Add text, boxes, arrows, pixelated areas and animated zoom, each with its own start and end. Export and it’s copied.

E opens the editor

Record and share

Drag a region of the screen and record with system audio and the microphone of your choice. Encoded by the graphics card, up to 120 fps. Instant replay saves the last seconds when something happens.

  • Win Shift R Record the screen
  • Win Shift G Save the replay
  • Alt Z Recording panel

Questions

What people usually ask.

What is Zas Player?

Zas (or Zas Player) is a free video player for Windows 10 and 11 that opens instantly. It has a screen recorder, a video trimmer, a clip editor and a photo viewer built in.

How do I download Zas?

Click “Download Zas” on this page: it downloads the official installer, Zas-Setup.exe, from GitHub. Open it, it installs in seconds and from then on it updates itself when you say so.

Can Zas record my screen?

Yes. Press Win+Shift+R, drag a region and record with system audio and the microphone of your choice, up to 120 fps and encoded by the graphics card. Instant replay saves the last seconds with Win+Shift+G, and Alt+Z opens the recording panel.

Is it free? Does it have ads?

It’s free, with no ads and no account to create.

What formats does it open?

Almost any video (MP4, MKV, WebM, MOV, AVI, WMV, TS…) thanks to mpv and FFmpeg, and photos in JPEG, PNG, WebP, HEIC, AVIF and more.
